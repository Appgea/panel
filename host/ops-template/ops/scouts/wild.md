Lens: the one lens with no list. It is after a kind of problem that is not in `scouts/_classes.md`.
The other lenses find the same kinds in app after app, and those become checks. This one widens the list.

1. The run names the app. Read its README and `apps/<key>.md`.
2. Read `scouts/_classes.md` in full. Every kind there is known: finding one more of it is not this run's job.
3. Pick one way in that `## Learned` below does not name for this app. Some, and you may invent one:
   - A first-time user, from the store page to the first screen that asks for money. What confuses, what waits, what lies.
   - Someone back after 30 days, after an update, on a new phone, or after a reinstall. What is gone, what is stale.
   - Break it: no network, a slow one, the clock moved, a full disk, a call mid-task, the app killed mid-save, a
     purchase interrupted, two devices on one account, a language switched mid-session.
   - One whole flow read as its user, start to end across files: every promise a screen makes against the code behind it.
   - What the app keeps about a person, where it goes, and what happens when they ask to leave.
   - The last 50 reviews and support mails, read for what people could not do, not for what crashed.
   - The numbers: a price, a reward, an odds table, a timer, a limit. Does the economy survive a user who tries.
4. Walk the path in the code, screen by screen, as the user would meet it.
5. Stay on that one way in until you can prove 1 to 3 findings, or can say why it holds.

A finding: something a user loses, proven by a path they can take, of a kind `scouts/_classes.md` does not have.
Add the kind to `scouts/_classes.md` in the same run.
Not a finding: one more case of a known kind. Name it in your note and move on.
Close every run with a `## Learned` line here: "<app>: took <the way in>, <what it showed>", so the next run takes another way.
