Decided

What I decided for you

You asked me to decide. Each line can be overruled.

  • Live is a new section of Panel, next to Review. It is the first step of moving Panel's UI to Bevy, one section at a time. Nothing else is cut.
  • Rooms are projects, not harnesses. You think in apps, money and stores. The harness is a letter on the dot.
  • Only business reaches you: Decide, Decided, Done, Better and Talk. No permission prompts.
  • Talk is on every card. It opens a conversation with the agent behind it, with the card attached.
  • Every motion is a playbook entry that says what it tells you. Haiku picks an entry for new events and proposes new animations.
  • Every dot opens a panel. Every stopped agent leaves an ember that holds its report.
  • Light means state. Words sit on quiet panels, as your quiet Mac rule from #1158 says.
Look

Light means state

Words sit still.

  • Glow is only for dots, beams and threads, and it always means a state. Nothing glows for decoration.
  • Every word sits on a quiet panel: flat, grey, one accent, Title Case labels, no hover lifts. That is today's Panel style rule, kept as is.
  • No XP, streaks, quests or scores. The game feel comes from a living world you can read at a glance.
  • A working dot glows still. It pulses once when it starts a new step. A world where nothing changes draws no frames.
  • Live is always dark. Glow needs a dark room.
Attention

What reaches you

Business only. Each card answers one of your needs, and Talk is on every card.

Decide
A business call only you can make: price, scope, store, what a product is, final art. These are your agenda, the open you asks. Your tap writes the same Answer: line a Slack tap writes, through board answer.
Decided
Calls agents made in your place, each with its why. Today they scroll by in #news. Here they come as one card per project: keep them all, undo one, or talk.
Done
What got done since you last looked, per project: issues closed, releases sent, attempts that failed. The reports are one click away.
Better
What could be done better, with a proposal: a step that keeps losing time, a test that keeps failing, the same bug twice. Do it files the work, because you asked.
Talk
An agent wants your view before it goes on. The box types into its session, with the card attached. A stopped agent's session is resumed.

Permission prompts never reach you. Agents run in auto mode. A prompt that hangs shows as a red stuck dot, which Mahmut Hoca handles.

The order

priority = stake × (1 + wait ÷ 60) ÷ minutes

stake
App weight times kind weight. App: 3 for a live app that earns, 2 for a live app, 1 for apps in progress and tools. Kind: Decide 3, Better 2, Talk 2, Decided 1, Done 1.
wait
Minutes since the card arrived. A low card still rises, so nothing starves.
minutes
What it costs you. Decide 1, Better 1.5, Talk 2, Decided 0.5, Done 1. Live times your real answers and replaces these guesses.

This is Smith's rule from scheduling: value per minute of your time, highest first. A one-minute price call for Tavla comes before a long read about a tool.

Rules

  • Decided and Done come as one card per project, never one per item.
  • The card in front of you never changes under your hand. New cards line up behind it.
  • Only a Decide card on a stop may ping you, and never in quiet hours. The rest wait.
  • The queue says how long it will take, like “6 cards · about 7 min”.
  • An empty queue dims your ring, and the top line says nothing needs you. You can leave.
  • Rooms never move and the recommended answer is always in the same spot, so your eyes and hand learn the map.

Dot

Reading a dot

Eight signals, no reading needed.

Size is the model. Opus or Fable, Sonnet or Sol, Haiku or Flash.

Colour is the state. Working, thinking, waits on you, idle, stuck.

The letter is the harness. C Claude Code, X Codex, G Grok, O OpenCode, M Gemini, K Kimi.

Small dots circling it are its subagents. They fly back in when they finish.

A beam flashes when one agent starts another. Hover a dot to see its family.

An amber thread runs from a room or an agent to your ring while its card waits.

An ember on the shelf is an agent that stopped. Click it for the report.

Where it stands is how far its work is: Plan, Build, Check or Ship.

  • Working. Cyan, still, one pulse per new step.
  • Planning. Blue and still.
  • Wants to talk. Amber blink, thread to you.
  • Idle. Grey and still.
  • Stuck or failed. Red, slow blink.
  • Done. A green flash, then an ember.

Names show on hover, on the selected dot, on a dot that wants to talk, and on a stuck dot. The rest stay quiet.

Playbook

Animations that mean something

Every motion is a playbook entry. Haiku picks one for anything new.

An entry names its trigger, what moves, what it tells you, how long it lasts and how often it may play. An entry that cannot say what it tells you does not ship.

The director

  • Every event goes to plain rules first. A known kind plays its entry at once.
  • An unknown kind goes to Haiku in a batch every 30 seconds. Haiku answers with an entry and its settings, with “too small to show”, or with a new animation idea: name, trigger, what moves and what it tells you.
  • Each answer is saved for that kind, so a new kind costs one call, once.
  • New ideas join the playbook's proposals. An agent builds them, the taste gate checks them, and you see a short clip only when the gate is unsure.
  • Haiku also writes each agent's line in business words, like “Fixing the reconnect bug that drops online matches”. At most one line per agent every 3 minutes.
  • A daily cap on Haiku calls shows on This Mac. Without Haiku, Live still draws everything from rules.
Agent

The agent panel

What it is doing and what it has done, one click away.

  • Hover shows a peek: name, state and the current step.
  • Click opens the panel beside the dot. A thin line ties it to the dot. You can pin several.
Title
Name, harness, model, project, running time.
Task
The board issue, or the first line of your prompt.
Now
The tool it runs, with a timer. “Running flutter test · 0:42”.
Started by
Its parent, one click away. You, Mahmut Hoca, board work or a script.
Family
Its subagents and the workers it started.
Done so far
Each step, newest first: files edited, tests, commits, subagents, your answers. A file opens in your editor. A commit opens in your git tool.
Actions
Talk types into its terminal. Terminal opens the live terminal. Stop needs a one-second hold, so a slip never kills an agent.

Every row comes from what the agent already writes: its session log, the board, wip and git. Nothing new is asked of the agents.

Reports

When an agent stops

It leaves an ember. The ember holds its report.

  • The report is built the moment the process ends, from facts only. The first line of its last message, its board result, its wip done notes, its commits and files, the pictures it sent, its time and tokens.
  • The outcome is one of five: Done, Failed, Stopped, Crashed, Stuck.
  • Crashed means the process vanished with no last message. The report then lists the files it left busy in wip.
  • No AI call. It costs nothing and is ready at once.
  • Embers glow on the room's shelf for 12 hours. Then the report lives in the Logbook, searchable by project, agent and day.
  • A report an agent files through Panel's own report tool joins the same card.
Rooms

Rooms are projects

You think in apps. The building is your portfolio.

  • Each project with an agent in the last 24 hours gets a room. The rest sit in the Quiet row and wake into a room when an agent starts there.
  • At most eight rooms show in full, the busiest by stake. The rest shrink to one line with their dots inline.
  • HQ is the ops folder. Mahmut Hoca, the scouts, board runs and the Manager live there. Their beams into app rooms show fleets at work.
  • Elsewhere holds agents outside any project, like a session in your home folder.
  • Rooms keep fixed places in portfolio order. You can drag them once. They never reshuffle.
  • The room sign shows Now, one line per agent, and Last, the latest stopped agent's result. Its panel adds open issues and yesterday's revenue from RevenueCat for apps that earn.
  • An amber edge marks a room with a card for you. A red edge marks a room with a stuck agent.
  • This Mac sits at the base: heat, CPU, the two testlock bays with who holds them, and each harness's weekly quota.
Move

Moving to Bevy, section by section

Live goes first. Nothing else is cut.

  • Live is a new row in Panel's sidebar, next to Review. appgeapanel://live opens it.
  • Inside the section, Bevy draws. It runs as WebAssembly in Panel's own window, on WebGPU, or WebGL2 where WebGPU is missing.
  • The world model runs natively in Panel's Rust backend and streams to the section.
  • Text boxes, like Talk, are plain HTML over the Bevy canvas. Typing Turkish just works.
  • The same Bevy code runs natively later, when the shell itself moves.
  • Next in line: Review, then Reports, then the terminal wall. A section moves only when its Bevy version is faster and you like it more.
Out

Kept out

Each of these would cost you attention, money or trust.

  • No XP, streaks, quests, levels or scores.
  • No glow on text and no glow for decoration.
  • No prompts, raw logs or command text on screen. Secrets live there. Labels are one line and scrubbed.
  • No permission prompts or technical yes or no in your queue.
  • No AI in the core loop. Rules draw everything. Haiku only names new kinds and writes plain lines, batched, saved and capped.
  • No fake motion. Every dot is a real process. A still dot is idle.
  • No layout that reshuffles.
  • No ping per event. Only a Decide card on a stop may ping you.
  • No characters, faces or look taken from a known game.
  • This Mac only for now. Other Macs come later.
Build

How it is built

All Rust. The same engine crates, a new face.

Kept

  • panel-core, panel-ingress, panel-terminal, panel-store. About 2.4k lines with no UI in them.

New

  • panel-world finds every agent on the Mac and keeps rooms, dots, beams and events as one live model.
  • panel-attention builds the cards from your agenda, the Decided lines, closes and slow steps, and orders them with one pure function. It gets the one test, because a wrong order is the bug that costs you most.
  • panel-reports builds the report when a process ends.
  • panel-director holds the playbook, plays known kinds by rule and asks Haiku about new ones.
  • panel-live is the Bevy app, built to WebAssembly and loaded by the Live section.
  • Ingress stops dropping agents Panel did not start. They go to their project room, or to Elsewhere.
  • Board, fleets and Mahmut Hoca pass a parent tag to every agent they start. Today board.py work runs under launchd, so the process list alone loses who started it.

Where the facts come from

  • The process list every 2 seconds: who runs, and who started whom.
  • Claude session logs and their subagent logs, read as they grow. Codex session logs the same way.
  • Fleet state files: which issue each worker holds and how it ended.
  • wip logs: who edits which file, and the done notes.
  • The board, at most every 5 minutes: issue titles, your agenda of open you asks, and the Decided: lines of closed issues.
  • Panel hooks, when Panel hosts the agent: instant step changes and the end of each turn.
  • testlock for the bays. The macOS thermal state for heat.
  • Panel is public. The Appgea sources, fleets, wip and the board, load as a module. Everyone else still gets a working Live from the process list and session logs.

Budgets

  • Nothing changing: no frames drawn. Still glow costs nothing. Only changes animate.
  • Animating: up to 30 frames a second while Live is open, none when another section is open.
  • Haiku: a daily cap on calls, shown on This Mac.
  • The watcher stays under 3% of one core.
  • First frame in under 1 second.
  • IBM Plex Sans and Mono, bundled. Open license, every Turkish letter, the same look on Mac and Windows.
Risk

Tested first

Four things could break the plan. Each gets a spike before any art.

  • Bevy in Panel's window. Test: Bevy's glow on WebGPU in this Mac's WebKit and in Windows WebView2, with WebGL2 as the fallback.
  • Load time. The WebAssembly file must start in under a second from disk.
  • Who started whom. Needs the parent tag in board, fleets and Mahmut Hoca's prompt. Without it, a beam starts from HQ, marked unknown.
  • Thin logs. Grok, OpenCode, Gemini and Kimi show from the process list first. Their panels fill in as we learn to read their logs.
  • Noisy ideas. Haiku may propose motion nobody needs. The “what it tells you” line and the taste gate filter it.
